Why it is called StewardBox

Open with this

Lead with the name. It buys goodwill before a single screen is shown, and it tells the room what the app is faster than any feature list.

“We already have a box. Every Sunday the offering goes into it, someone counts it, and someone writes it down. That box is trusted — nobody in this church has ever doubted the box.

What we have never had is the part after the box. The counting sheet, the text message, the notebook, the Excel file on somebody's laptop. That is where things get lost.

So I built the second half of the box, and I called it StewardBox.”

The two halves of the name

HalfWhat it meansWhat to say
Steward A steward does not own the money — they answer for it. “It is required of stewards that they be found faithful.” (1 Cor. 4:2) “This app is not about control. It is about being able to answer for every peso, without anyone having to remember.”
Box The physical offering box the church already uses. Also: a small, closed, ordinary thing. Not a “system”. “You already know how to use a box. You drop something in and it is safe. That is the whole promise.”

The mascot, in one line

StewardBox is a wooden offering box with a green visor, a coin slot on top, and a cross on the front — the character on the sign-in screen and the phone header. He is a bookkeeper, not a robot: dependable, cheerful, always on duty.

Use him during the demo. He changes with the screen — arms out counting on the denomination calculator, arms crossed and waiting when there is no signal, visor popped and celebrating when entries finally sync. If someone smiles at that, you have won the room. People adopt software they feel affection for.

What not to say about the name

Don't call it a “financial management information system” — you lose them. Don't say it replaces anyone; it replaces paper, not the treasurer. Don't over-explain the theology: one verse, one sentence, move on.

Know your room

Three silent questions

Three different people are listening, and each is asking a different silent question. Answer all three, or one of them will quietly block adoption.

WhoTheir real questionThe moment that answers it
Pastor & deacons“Can I trust what this tells the congregation?”Activity Log — nothing can be deleted, every change has a name on it
Treasurer“Is this more work for me, or less?”Google Sheets sync and Reports — the numbers arrive already added up
Collectors“Will I look stupid using this?”The phone form and denomination calculator — three taps, in Taglish

If only one of these people is in the room, cut the demo down to their act and keep the rest in your back pocket.

The run of show

Six acts · 24 min
01

The problem

2 min · no screens

Screens off. Talk to the room. If you open with features, they evaluate features. If you open with the problem, they evaluate whether you understood their church.

“Right now, a Sunday collection touches about six people before it becomes a number anybody can look at. It gets counted, written on a sheet, texted to someone, typed into Excel, and then at the end of the year the treasurer reconstructs the whole thing from memory and photos.

Nothing there is dishonest. It is just fragile. One phone dies, one sheet gets wet, one person is on leave, and we lose a Sunday.”

Then one line of transition:

“StewardBox takes that six-person chain and makes it two taps on the phone of whoever counted the money.”

02

The collector's phone

6 min · phoneKey moment

This act decides adoption. Everything else is for the leaders; this one is for the people who will actually touch it every week. Hand the phone to a collector if one is in the room and let them do it. Say nothing while they find the buttons — their success in silence is your best argument.

  1. Sign in with the email and password the admin gave them. Point out: no setup, no app store, no install. It is a link.
  2. Submit tab opens by default. Note the three tabs at the bottom — Submit Recent Summary. That is the whole app on mobile.
  3. Collection / Expense toggle at the top. “Money in, money out. Same form.”
  4. Pick the date the money was collected — not today's date. This matters, and it is the number one mistake to warn about.
  5. Cash or GCash. Mention that GCash is offered only for General Tithes & Offering, because that is the only thing people actually send that way.
  6. Type the amounts beside each fund — General Tithes & Offering, Sisterhood (San Juan), Sisterhood (Labuin), Brotherhood, Youth, Couples, Sunday School, Special Purpose / Pledge, Bank Interest. Blank means nothing came in for it.
  7. Control number, cheque number and forms number are optional. Say this out loud — collectors freeze on fields they can't fill.
  8. Check the total at the bottom, then tap Submit.
The moment that always lands

Open the denomination calculator. Type the number of pieces beside each denomination — 1000, 500, 200, 100, 50, 20, 10, 5, 1 — and let the room watch the total climb by itself. Then push that total into the form.

“Nobody wants to add ₱1,000 + ₱500 + ₱200 in their head on a Sunday with people waiting. This is the part that saves the most time. It also removes the most common error, which is not dishonesty — it is arithmetic.”

03

The airplane-mode moment

3 min · phoneThey remember this

Set it up first: “The church hall has bad signal. Everyone in this room knows that. So let me show you what happens when there is no internet.” Then turn on airplane mode in front of them and submit a collection exactly as before.

  1. It submits. No error, no lost typing.
  2. An amber number appears on the Recent tab — that is what is still waiting to be sent.
  3. Turn airplane mode off. Wait. The number clears by itself.

“Nobody has to remember to re-send anything. The phone does it when the signal comes back.”

State this clearly — it is the only real risk

“The one rule: do not uninstall the app or clear its data while there are entries still waiting. If the amber number is showing, those entries are on that phone and nowhere else yet.”

While you are on the Recent tab, show two more things:

  • Pending vs. sent — a pending entry has not reached the office yet. No mark means it is saved. A failed entry has a retry button.
  • Supplements — tap a record, tap the supplement option. The form reopens with the same date and the other payment method already selected. “Someone gave ₱500 by GCash after you already submitted the cash. This is that.”
04

The Sunday message

3 min · phone

The collectors will love this one, because it removes a chore they currently do by hand every Sunday night.

  1. Tap the Summary tab.
  2. Tap the Sunday on the calendar. Only dates that already have records can be tapped — a greyed-out date means nothing was recorded, not that the app is broken.
  3. The message appears — every fund on its own line, the total at the bottom, the date heading formatted properly. You can also select a date range when you need to cover more than one Sunday.
  4. Type in the attendance line yourself if the church posts it.
  5. Copy message → open Messenger → paste. Do the paste live and let them see it land in a real chat.

“That message used to be typed out by hand every Sunday night. It is now a copy and a paste — and the numbers in it cannot disagree with the records, because they are the records.”

Explain before they ask

GCash gets its own line in the message even though it is tithes. That is how the church has always posted it.

05

The treasurer's desk

6 min · laptop

Switch to the laptop and change tone. This half is for the people who answer to the congregation. Walk the left menu top to bottom — it is grouped, and the groups are the story.

Overview

  • Dashboard — the boxes across the top: received, spent, what is left, for the month and year selected. Change the month picker in front of them so they see everything redraw. Say: “If a page looks empty, check the month first. It almost always means nothing was entered that month.”
  • Analytics — collections against expenses, side by side, month over month. Hover a bar for the exact peso amount. A taller expense bar is a month the church spent more than it received.
  • Reports — three figures, defined precisely, because these are the numbers read aloud at a meeting.
FigureWhat it counts
Collections TotalEvery peso received in the selected period
Expenses TotalEvery peso spent in the same period
Net SurplusCollections minus Expenses
Say this before someone panics at a red number

“A negative Net Surplus is not an error in the app. It means that month we spent more than we received.”

Management

  • Manage Records — Collections and Expenses tabs. Edit a record: change an amount and point out that the total recalculates itself. Prefer editing over deleting — editing keeps the history. New entries come from the phone, not from here.
  • Users — add a collector, set their role, hand them the password. Removing access lives here too, for when someone leaves the church.
  • Mobile Form Fields — the underrated one. This is how the church changes the app without a programmer. Switch a fund off and it disappears from every collector's phone immediately; switch it on and it comes back. Records already submitted keep their values.

“If the church starts a new fund next year, nobody has to call me. The admin turns it on.”

Actions & Help

  • Sunday Collection — the same message flow as the phone, for whoever prefers a keyboard.
  • User Guide — show it, and make a point of it. Every screen you just demoed is written up in plain steps, with Taglish notes on the parts people actually get stuck on. The guide only shows what that person's role can do, so a collector is never shown admin instructions.

“Nobody has to remember today. It is all in the app.”

06

Why you can trust it

4 min · laptopThe closer

Close on trust. This is what the leaders are actually buying.

RoleCan doGive it to
UserSend collections and expenses from the phoneMost collectors
AdminEverything a User can, plus manage records, users and phone form fieldsTreasurer, finance secretary
Super AdminEverything an Admin can, plus the Activity Log. Only a Super Admin can create another AdminOne or two people, no more

“When you are unsure, give someone User. You can raise them later. You cannot un-see what a wider role showed them.”

The Activity Log

Best done live: edit a record in Manage Records, then open the Activity Log and find the line you just made.

  1. Every line shows the date and time, who, and what they did.
  2. Click the line — it opens to show which values changed, from what to what.
  3. Then say the sentence that ends the demo.

“Nothing on this page can be edited or deleted. Not by me, not by the treasurer, not by the pastor. That is the entire point of it. If a number in this church ever changes, this page says who changed it and what it was before.”

Then Google Sheets, briefly

Give the treasurer their spreadsheet — don't fight it. Reports page → Google Sheets section → paste the sheet link → Sync. It creates a tab for the year and fills it with the records.

The one gotcha

The sheet must be shared as Editor, not Viewer. That is the cause of nearly every failed sync.

“You keep your spreadsheet. You just stop typing into it.”

The hard questions

Short beats correct

Prepare these. They will come, and a confident short answer is worth more than a correct long one.

“What if the internet is down?”

Already demoed it. “It saves on the phone and sends itself later. You saw it.”

“What if someone loses their phone?”

“Nothing is stored on the phone except entries that haven't sent yet. Remove their access from the Users page and that account is done.”

“Can someone change the numbers without us knowing?”

“An admin can correct a record — they have to be able to, people mistype. But every correction is written into the Activity Log with their name, the old value and the new value. And that log cannot be edited by anyone.”

“Does this replace the treasurer?”

“No. It replaces the treasurer's typing. They still decide, still report, still answer to you. They just stop re-encoding numbers somebody else already counted.”

“We already have our Excel file.”

“Keep it. The app syncs into Google Sheets. The difference is the numbers arrive already added up, on Sunday, instead of being typed in at the end of the month.”

“What if we want to add a new fund?”

“Mobile Form Fields. Toggle it on. It's on every collector's phone within seconds. No programmer.”

“Who can see the money?”

“Only accounts an admin creates. There is no public sign-up. Sign-in is by password or Google, and the roles decide what each person sees.”

“How much does it cost, and who maintains it?”

Answer honestly with your own arrangement. Do not improvise a number in the room — say you will put it in writing.

“What if you leave, or get busy?”

Take this seriously; it is the most legitimate objection. Answer with three things: Mobile Form Fields (the church changes its own funds), the in-app User Guide (the church trains its own people), and the Google Sheets sync (the data lives somewhere the church already owns).

Closing and next steps

Leave with three names

Do not end on “any questions?”. End on a proposal.

“I am not asking you to switch today. I am asking for one month, in parallel. Keep doing it exactly the way you do now. The collectors also submit here. At the end of the month we compare, line by line. If it matches, we stop doing it twice. If it doesn't, I fix it.”

Then ask for exactly three things:

WhoWho is the Super Admin? One name, decided in the room.
WhichWhich collectors get accounts first? Two or three, not everyone.
WhenWhich Sunday do we start? A date, in the room.
Why this matters

Nobody leaves a meeting and sets this up on their own. Leave with three names and a date, or the demo was theatre.
